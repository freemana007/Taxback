Get your tax back in 6 steps

Most people who work for an employer pay too much tax because they never claim what they're owed. You don't need to know anything about tax. Follow these steps in order. Each one tells you exactly what to look for and where.

Working on tax year . Change the year at the top to work on another one.

    What can I claim?

    Answer yes or no. Every “yes” is money you can probably get back.

      How to claim your refund

      Revenue has two websites for claiming. Pick the one that fits you.

        Words you'll see

        What your claims are worth

        Tax saved by each relief for the selected year, based on your income and receipts.

        Tax calculation

        Income tax, USC and PRSI

        Everything taken from your pay for the year. Tax claims only reduce income tax; USC and PRSI don't change.

        All open years

        You can claim back up to 4 years. Each year closes on 31 December, four years later.

        Add a receipt

        You pay this yourself, so your insurer already took 20% off the price. There is nothing more to claim, and you don't need to add it.

        How do you want to enter your pay?

        Read a payslip

        Pick a PDF payslip and the app finds the pay and tax on it. It's read on this device only; the file isn't saved or sent anywhere.

        Pay for each month

        Copy “Taxable pay” (or “Gross pay”), “PAYE” (income tax), “USC” and “PRSI” from each payslip. Only pay and income tax are needed. Paid weekly? Type each week with + between, like 812 + 812 + 812 + 812.

        USC and PRSI

        The app works these out from your pay. Add what you paid to see if you paid the right amount. All optional.

        Other details

        Your data

        Everything is stored only in this browser. Nothing is uploaded. Export a backup now and then, especially before clearing your browser.

        Rates used

        Years marked “check” use figures that should be confirmed on revenue.ie. Figures live in tax.js.

        What this does not cover

        • PRSI for the self-employed (Class S) and the USC surcharge on self-employed income over €100,000. Employee PRSI is worked out on your average week, so it can differ by a few euro from your payslips.
        • Self-assessed (Form 11) income, rental income, Rent-a-Room relief, Help to Buy.
        • Separate or single assessment of married couples, widowed or single-parent credits.
        • Dependent relative credit and other less common credits.

        This is an estimate to help you organise your claims. It isn't tax advice; check anything sizeable with Revenue or an accountant.
